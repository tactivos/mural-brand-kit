Logo assets

The logo exists in two forms: a wordmark (full name) and a symbol (stylized M). Use the wordmark for big brand moments where audiences need to know our name. Use the symbol where it’s already clear the communication is coming from Mural.

The wordmark is rooted in “structure and play” — black bars complemented by five core colors that suggest collaboration, polyphony, and individuality. The symbol is a collapsed version: a singular “M” containing all five colors and the two foundational black bars.

Pairing rules

Always use STK Bureau Serif for headlines and ABC Social for body copy. Never use ABC Social without STK Bureau Serif in marketing applications.

Pair them in: two-part statements (headline + answer), editorial content (website, blog), and complex documents where differentiating content types improves readability.

Headline spacing

Tracking: Tighten by −10 to −100px at large sizes (51pt+). Use letter-spacing: -1.2px for standard headlines.

Leading rule: Tracking < Word spacing < Leading (always). Headlines: line-height: 1.1. Body: line-height: 1.55.

Never justify headlines. Never apply effects (3D, drop shadow, warp) to any text. Always use smart typography: curly quotes (“ ”), em dashes (—), and proper ellipses (…).

Writing rules

Do

  • Get to the point — be direct with clear takeaways
  • Demonstrate value — show it, don’t just talk about it
  • Keep it simple — no ten-dollar words
  • Speak your customers’ language
  • Differentiate — if a competitor could say it, don’t
  • Focus on results with specifics
  • Take a stance on what’s broken
  • Put the customer first, always

Don’t

  • Make Mural the hero — the customer is
  • Use jargon or overly technical terms
  • Use passive voice or hesitant language
  • Treat every audience the same
  • Use vague positive statements
  • Be afraid to show personality

Punctuation

We use AP Style at Mural with some exceptions. When in doubt, refer to the AP style guide.

Em dashes (—), en dashes (–), and hyphens (-)

  • Em dashes set off clauses for emphasis — like this — with no spaces around them at Mural.
  • En dashes connect ranges: “9–5,” “pages 1–10.”
  • Hyphens join compound modifiers before a noun (“a well-known brand”) but not after (“the brand is well known”). Don’t hyphenate adverbs ending in -ly (“a newly released feature”).

Ellipses (…)

Use sparingly, and only in the middle of a sentence. Use three dots, no spaces before the ellipsis.

Commas (,)

Always use the Oxford comma.

  • Do: “eggs, honey, and sugar”
  • Don’t: “eggs, honey and sugar”

Colons (:)

Capitalize the first word after a colon only if it begins a proper noun or a complete sentence.

  • Capitalized: “Punctuation: We pronounce it…”
  • Not capitalized: “Three words: time, dedication, and tenacity”

Exclamation points (!)

Don’t overuse. Limit to one exclamation point.

“And” versus ampersand (&)

Always use “and.” Ampersands are acceptable only in company names, product names, and presentations where the design calls for it.

Lists

Keep bullets short — use them for overviews, not full paragraphs. Capitalize the first word in each item. Use periods for complete sentences; omit them for fragments. Use numbered lists for sequential or prioritized items.

Punctuation placement in quotation marks

Periods and commas always go inside quotation marks. Colons and semicolons go outside. Question marks and exclamation points go inside if part of the quote, outside if not.

  • “How are you doing?” Philip asked.
  • Did the dog bite every time they said “go away” at the door?

Numbers, dates, & times

We use AP Style at Mural, with some exceptions. When in doubt, refer to the AP Style Guide.

Numbers

Spell out one through nine. Use numerals for 10 and up — except at the beginning of a sentence. Numerics are acceptable in social media, ad spaces, and blog/webinar titles where they may help with character count or click-through rates.

Dates

Use Arabic figures without st, nd, rd, or th. Capitalize month names in all uses. With a specific date, abbreviate only Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., and Dec. Spell out when used alone or with a year alone. Month + year: no comma. Month + day + year: set off the year with commas.

Examples:

  • Mural was founded in 2011.
  • On May 13, 2021, Mural celebrated its 10th anniversary.
  • The next webinar on hybrid learning will take place at 11 am PT on Friday, Sept. 15.
  • I have created six murals this month, but next month I will create 20 canvases for the big PI Planning convention.

Times

Format as HH:MM am/pm ET/PT/etc. Don’t include minutes for times on the hour.

Writing about people

Names: Full name + title on first reference. First name only after that.

Job titles: Capitalize before a name (“President Smith”). Lowercase after (“Lindsey is an enterprise transformation manager”).

Team names: “Our Support team” (capitalize team name, lowercase “team”).

Pronouns: Use preferred pronouns. Default to they/them if unknown. Use gender-inclusive language (mail carrier, server, humankind, first-year student, team capacity).

Accessibility

In recent years, web accessibility has become much more important for digital marketing. It may not be the sexiest topic, but we need to pay attention to make sure our writing is accessible to all audiences.

Headings

It’s important to use page structure to allow screen readers to accurately parse content. Use headings for each section of writing, and use the heading hierarchy. The correct tag is H1 for page titles and product names, H2 for sections, H3 for sub-sections. Always keep headings in sequential descending order. Headings should be descriptive, enabling users to navigate the content efficiently. Use headings, not bold text, for section breaks, and consider using a table of contents for longer content.

Organization

Keep writing short. Use a single event or idea per paragraph, and start a new paragraph before moving on to a different idea. Keep paragraphs short when you can.

Directions

Don’t give directions that rely on visual attributes alone, such as “click the green button on the right side of the screen” or “click the right/left arrow.” Use “navigate” or “return” instead.

Closed captions

Videos should have closed captions whenever possible. At the very least, the content included in the video should also be available in text.

Mobile optimization

Always test content on a mobile device before publishing to make sure it’s formatted correctly and easy to engage with.

Alt text

All images on our web properties (and our social media where possible) should have alt tags that describe the content of the image. This helps us communicate with both people who use screen readers and people who have slow internet connections or who use image blockers.

Calls to action

Calls to action should be specific, helpful, and actionable, whether they appear in the form of a button or link. They should still make sense out of context, with or without the text around them, and should each be distinct from one another. When there are several on a page, make sure to differentiate them — for example, instead of two “Read More” CTAs, be specific by saying “Read the case study.”

Usage guidelines

When to use

  • Presentation backgrounds for a collaborative feel

  • Behind product screenshots or UI mockups

Best practices

  • Keep dots subtle—they should be felt, not seen

  • Avoid placing dot grid over photos or complex visuals

Visual Rules

Do’s & don’ts

The most common visual rules at a glance. When in doubt, check here.

Do

Maintain the correct color order. Pink, Red, Green, Yellow, Blue — always left to right, with correct proportions.

Don’t

Reorder or equalize the brand bar. Scrambling the stripes or making them equal widths breaks brand recognition.

Do

Show the brand bar on black or white. All five colors remain fully visible.

Don’t

Place the brand bar on green. The green stripe vanishes into Jade.

Do

Alternate blocks with contrast. Jade + Black, White + Natural — always a visible edge.

Headline in Merriweather Body copy in Arial. Clean, readable, and on-brand.
Do

Pair Merriweather Light headlines with Arial body. Serif/sans-serif creates hierarchy.

Headline in Arial Bold Body copy in Merriweather. Reversed pairing feels off.
Don’t

Reverse the font pairing. Arial headlines with serif body breaks visual hierarchy.

Usage rules

Do

  • Always capitalize as LUMA (never “Luma”)
  • Make clear LUMA is a methodology within Mural
  • Use system colors only for their specific methods
  • Use LUMA’s own typography (Georgia/Arial digital, Charter/Helvetica print)

Don’t

  • Mix LUMA colors with Mural logo palette
  • Interchange LUMA Red (#EE3A43) with Mural Red (#FF4B4B)
  • Use Mural typography (STK Bureau Serif / ABC Social) for LUMA materials
  • Use system colors outside of method-specific contexts
